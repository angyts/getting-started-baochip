# I²S — digital audio data

**In one sentence:** I²S carries digital audio samples from a processor to an audio converter or amplifier using a bit clock, a left/right frame clock and a serial data line.

I²S is not the same thing as I²C. Their names look similar, but they are different buses for different jobs.

## The usual signals

- **BCLK:** bit clock; marks individual data-bit timing.
- **LRCLK / WS:** left/right or word-select clock; marks which audio channel is being transmitted.
- **DIN:** digital audio data into the receiving device.
- Some systems also use MCLK, but whether it is needed depends on the audio chip. Do not assume it is required or absent without checking the codec/amplifier datasheet.

## Baochip documentation entry

The autogenerated Baochip register index contains one [UDMA_I2S block](https://ci.betrusted.io/bao1x/udma_i2s.html). This is the register-level reference for the I²S peripheral.

The introductory book does not provide a full I²S driver chapter or a step-by-step playback example. The existence of a UDMA_I2S register group alone does not establish a ready-to-use audio driver in a particular Xous build; check the relevant HAL and service source.

## Related but different: BIO

BIO is a programmable I/O coprocessor. It can be programmed to generate custom timed signals, and is used in the Xous examples for protocols such as SPI bit-banging and LED signalling.

BIO is not the same hardware block as UDMA_I2S. A custom BIO-based I²S transmitter is a software-defined solution; UDMA_I2S is a dedicated peripheral register block. The correct choice depends on the available Xous driver, required audio format and pins, and the hardware's exact capabilities.

For audio experiments, validate BCLK, LRCLK and DIN with a logic analyser at the receiving chip. A completed BIO FIFO transfer or an application-level “playback complete” message is not proof that a valid audio waveform reached the amplifier.
