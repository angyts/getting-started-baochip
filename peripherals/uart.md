# UART — serial communication

**In one sentence:** UART sends and receives bytes over a transmit wire and a receive wire, without a separate clock wire.

UART is commonly used for:
- A console or debug terminal.
- Communication with a GPS or modem.
- Simple serial modules.
- Bootloader or diagnostic links.

## How it works

The sender and receiver agree on settings such as baud rate, data bits, parity and stop bits. Each side uses its own clock to interpret the timing. Because there is no shared clock wire, both sides must agree closely on the serial settings.

The usual signal names are:
- TX: data sent out by this device.
- RX: data received by this device.
- GND: common reference.

## Practical checks

- Confirm TX on one device connects to RX on the other, and vice versa.
- Connect a common ground.
- Confirm I/O voltage compatibility.
- Match baud rate and framing.
- Do not connect two outputs together.
